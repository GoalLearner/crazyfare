CrazyFare checklist

October 28, 2026, 2:30 PM PST. Built from three videos you sent, checked against the game as it is live today (v16). Tick boxes as you go; they stay ticked in this browser.

! Are we on track?

The game itself: yes. All three videos say the same things win on Roblox: play with friends, something to grind and show off, a reason to come back tomorrow, and a skill to get good at. CrazyFare already has all of that. It has 6 players in one city, bots, items, ramming, bounties, levels, upgrades, 9 cars, quests, leaderboards, crates, the Phantom, weekly events and a coach.

Getting players: no, and this is the big one. The two developer videos agree on one lesson above everything else: test with real players first, then build. The 20-minute video's game made money after a 2-week build; the same developer's 3-year, all-money project made nothing. CrazyFare has had 16 versions and zero visits. In Creator Hub today, the game is Private. The description is empty, no thumbnails are uploaded and there's no custom icon, even though the art is ready in docs/launch.

So the next move isn't more features. Open the game to players this week, make the store page clickable, watch day-1 retention, then build what the numbers ask for.

1 What the three videos teach

VideoMain lessons
Player psychology (5 min)Design backwards from five cravings: progression (visible growth), status (something rare to flex, like a big lifetime stat), FOMO (daily rewards, limited events, framed as "you'll lose this"), mastery (a system you can learn and get good at), and community (most solo games fail on Roblox). Study hit games: watch players, read comments, and map each trigger → action → reward.
A developer's road (9 min)First games flop; that's normal. Common rookie misses: API services off, UI not built for phones. What finally worked for him was core retention: progression, random rewards, leaderboards, something to grind. Players care about fun, not clean code. Feedback from a community is fuel. Some bugs only show up in live servers. An update can drop your player count, so watch analytics. Take responsibility and don't let the numbers rule your mood.
Roblox millionaires (20 min)The formula: clickable (a dramatic, unique name and look), social (play with or against friends), replayable (curiosity, like "at level 10 I need level 100 to see what's next"). Test first, then build. Round-based games with map voting. Money comes from daily rotating shops, coins and abilities. No ads needed when the game clicks, because TikTok and YouTubers drive discovery (a big streamer playing one game led to about 100M views). They pay artists for thumbnails and icons.

10 The long game

  • Expect the first public week to be quiet. Both developers had flops before a hit; judge by retention, not visits.
  • Small updates on a set day (Friday is planned in docs/LAUNCH.html), each one answering what players did.
  • If an update drops the numbers, roll back or fix fast. A bad week doesn't mean the game is over.
  • Rest. The millionaires work focused blocks and stop, and they say the breaks give them their best ideas.
